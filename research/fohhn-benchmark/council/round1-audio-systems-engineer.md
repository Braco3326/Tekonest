# Council round 1: Dr Hélène Marchetti (sound-system design engineer)

## Persona
Dr Hélène Marchetti: 18 years as a touring PA tech, then a consultant in France on installed sound and voice alarm (EASE, ArrayCalc, Soundvision, STIPA).
Physics first. My first question is always "which model, and how far off can it be?"

## Findings

Reasoning, in brief: the arithmetic tests in the ladder only *verify* the code against itself. Nothing *validates* the model against measurement. Errors on differences (between points, or between Essais) are much smaller than errors on absolute levels. So relative readouts are defensible at (b), and absolute ones mostly are not.

## Position
**C, gated, with stricter gates than the draft.** Phase 1 (B) ships first, with no conditions from me. Phase 2 (a) is fine once the geometry tests and the specified directivity formula (#4) are in place. Phase 3 (b) goes ahead only if:
- every level output is **relative** by default (to a reference point, or Δ between Essais); absolute dB appears only behind a "niveau supposé" drive with a stated spectrum;
- S1 is rescored on timing and arrival order (#8), and S3 is rewritten as relative or margin-based (#9);
- one measurement campaign (a real room, at least 5 points) is documented before any error bound is shown.

## Non-negotiables
1. No "±3 dB" (or any error bound) until it is validated against measurement.
2. No absolute dB(A) or dB(C) printed next to the decree limits; no LCeq estimate.
3. No computed STI value, per seat or per room, below (c).
4. No `sensitivityDb1W1m` for powered speakers unless it is published; drive is headroom below max SPL.
5. The S1 delay is computed from path difference at a named reference point, the precedence offset is bounded and sourced, and no level-based success criterion depends on delay time.
6. Every [L] formula (precedence, cone fit, Lw/DI) is sourced or derived in the ADR before it ships.
7. Labels on every result: "champ direct / moyenne statistique", "somme énergétique", "spectre supposé", fidelity badge.
