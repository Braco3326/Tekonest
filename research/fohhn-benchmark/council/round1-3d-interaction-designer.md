# Round 1 — Lena Vogt, 3D interaction designer

**Persona.** Principal 3D interaction designer: 14 years across CAD/BIM viewers, Unity/Babylon training sims and WebGL configurators.
Method: spatial task, then the fewest modes, every state visible. First question: *what is the user looking at, and how do they know it's current?*

## Position

**B now; C (capped at fidelity a, then b) only through gates.** Phase 1 teaches the method and needs only two new controls: "Figer" (G) and a compare toggle (B). It also tests the hardest UX problem, "what am I looking at, and is it current?", with no acoustic noise. Before any heatmap ships (phases 2–3), I need four things:
- the redrawn 1366×768 layout;
- the zone-top-surface rendering;
- the flip-plus-DOM compare;
- a frame-time measurement on a real school laptop.

## Non-negotiables

1. **Displayed vs live is always explicit.** The caption names what is shown (live, Essai X, A/B compare) and its freshness **in words**. A "Montage actuel" row is always present.
2. **No existing binding changes meaning by mode.** PageUp/PageDown stay look. New actions are remappable, labelled through `layoutLabels`, never shown as raw codes, and appear in H help.
3. **Every new action has a named on-screen button.** Keys are accelerators only, and the mouse-only scheme can do everything.
4. **Compare is read-only and costs no second 3D render.** It closes with Esc at step 3b, which is documented in ADR-0010 and CONTROLS.md.
5. **The viewport keeps ≥ 60 % of the width at 1366×768,** with at most one side panel open.
6. **The heatmap is unlit, colour-blind safe, and drawn on the zone's top surface at the stated ear height.** Stale results show "—" and never a readable ramp.
7. **A perf gate on real hardware comes before phase 2.** No phone mode without an export path. Essais are off in the exam by default.
