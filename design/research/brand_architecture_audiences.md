# Brand architecture and audience routing for a Paris multi-service cultural brand (Tekonest)

Scope note: about 20 search/fetch calls. Several pages could not be fetched (noted under Gaps). Everything under "Cited Findings" was verified on the linked page during this session. "Inferences" are my interpretation and are not sourced facts.

## Q1. Comparable organisations combining events, community, education and services, and how they architect the brand

### Takeaway
The verified comparables follow a "branded house" or "master brand as driver" pattern: one name holds events, media, education and partnerships (Red Bull Music Academy inside Red Bull; Boiler Room across streaming, events and merch; Collectif MU running venues and residencies). The well-known education-plus-events model (RBMA) was run by an agency and closed in 2019. A large branded platform can stop.

### Cited Findings
- Red Bull Music Academy was Red Bull's music programme. It offered lectures, workshops and branded concerts, and ran alongside Red Bull Radio. Both were shut down when Red Bull's partnership with the agency Yadastar, which had run the "culture program" since 1998, ended. Red Bull said it was "moving away from a strongly centralized approach" toward local country teams. — [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/say-goodbye-to-red-bull-music-academy-and-radio/)
- Boiler Room is described as a "popular streaming platform" whose revenue also comes from ticketed events, merchandise, apparel and brand partnerships (Pernod Ricard, Ballantine's). Founder Blaise Bellville. DICE acquired it about four years before it was sold to festival operator Superstruct Entertainment (reported 16 Jan 2025), and DICE stays its ticketing partner. — [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/boiler-room-acquired-festival-giant-superstruct-entertainment)
- Collectif MU (Paris) ran Garage MU, a small concert venue in the Goutte d'Or, for about 15 years. It then led La Station – Gare des Mines, which it describes as a "laboratoire convivial et festif consacré aux scènes musicales émergentes" (concerts, parties, exhibitions, screenings, artist residencies). Partners were Hydropathes, Berlinons Paris, Atelier Craft and Point Éphémère. It is a temporary site in SNCF's Sites Artistiques Temporaires programme. — [Paris.fr](https://www.paris.fr/pages/la-station-nouveau-lieu-alternatif-et-multiculturel-parisien-3799)
- The Île-de-France Region funds "Résidences artistiques et ateliers à la Station – Gare des Mines" and has a convention with Collectif MU for visual arts. Public funding sits alongside the club activity. — [Île-de-France](https://www.iledefrance.fr/tous-les-services/ma-region-pres-de-chez-moi/culture/arts-plastiques-numeriques-et-urbains/212381-residences-artistiques-et-ateliers-la-station-gare-des-mines); [Convention](https://www.iledefrance.fr/tous-les-services/ma-region-pres-de-chez-moi/culture/arts-plastiques-numeriques-et-urbains/171215-convention-avec-le-collectif-mu-pour-les-arts-visuels)
- Mawimbi (Paris) works as a collective and "nascent label" at the same time. Its DJs/producers play clubs and released a debut album (MWB-001) on Bandcamp, so label and party share one name. — [OkayAfrica](https://okayafrica.com/mawimbi-collective-club-music-ghetto-house-modern-highlife)
- Several SWANA collectives in Europe combine parties with education or archive work under one name. Berlin's Sahra runs parties and DJ workshops. AL Berlin is a café-bar-studio with an annual festival and weekly workshops, gigs and discussions. London's Majazz Project is an archival label and research journal that also runs club nights. — [CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Noise/The-SWANA-Events-Enriching-Europe-s-Music-Scene)
- Technopol (Paris) appears to run "Electronic Lab" professional sessions (e.g. "Programmation et enjeux de billetterie" on Shotgun) alongside the Techno Parade and Paris Electronic Week. This is a federation-type org mixing events and professional training. I saw it only in search result titles; the Technopol page itself could not be fetched. — [Shotgun listing](https://shotgun.live/en/events/programmation-et-enjeux-de-billetterie); [Music Week interview](https://musicweek.com/live/read/six-questions-with-tommy-vaudecrane-of-paris-electronic-week/077869)

### Inferences
- In every case above, the event/party brand is the "driver" and education, label or workshops are extensions under the same name. That fits a branded house with descriptive sub-labels (e.g. "Tekonest Nights", "Tekonest Network", "Tekonest Learn"), not invented sub-brand names.
- TekPractice is the only branch with its own coined name. It could stay an endorsed product ("TekPractice by Tekonest") if it might later reach users outside the brand (other BTS schools), which matches Aaker's "create and own a key association" criterion (see Q5).
- The RBMA closure shows a risk: when education depends on one sponsor or partner, it can stop. A small founder should keep the free learning branch cheap and self-sufficient.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the home-page routing of these organisations (nav labels, CTAs). The pages were not fetched visually.
- Not researched or fetched: French "tiers-lieux" with training (e.g. La REcyclerie, Ground Control, Les Grands Voisins/Plateau Urbain), French sound schools tied to events, and Technopol's own site (franco.wiki fetch failed).

## Q2. Paris collectives in Arab/SWANA/MENA, Afro and electronic crossover, and how they present themselves

### Takeaway
The Paris scene is already active: Radio Flouka, Beirut Electro Parade, Disco Dabké (multi-city), Mawimbi (Afro club) and touring Acid Arab. Self-descriptions centre on a city or diaspora story, a specific era or genre, and a political or cultural mission, not generic "oriental" imagery. To stand out, Tekonest needs its own angle, such as the cross-regional Maghreb-to-Levant + Afro mix, or fair pay and technician focus.

### Cited Findings
- Radio Flouka is an international online radio station featuring Middle Eastern artists. It hosts events in Paris (e.g. at Le Chinois) and with Belgium's Yalla collective. It aims to redefine how the world sees contemporary music from the region. — [CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Noise/The-SWANA-Events-Enriching-Europe-s-Music-Scene)
- Radio Flouka is described as "a revolutionary cultural, political, and musical hub in Paris" whose beats "tell the MENA story". Only the page metadata loaded, not the full interview. — [Black Rhino Radio](https://blackrhinoradio.com/interviews/radio-flouka-parisian-echoes-of-the-mena)
- Radio Flouka runs open airs with Corps Libre at Le Hasard Ludique (Paris), listed on the City of Paris agenda, and has appeared on the Nuits Sonores (Lyon) lineup. — [Paris.fr](https://www.paris.fr/evenements/open-air-radio-flouka-x-corps-libre-67733); [Nuits Sonores](https://nuits-sonores.com/en/artiste/radio-flouka-2/)
- Beirut Electro Parade (Paris) was founded in 2017 by Hadi Zeidan after he moved to Paris and missed Beirut's scene. It plays electronic lineups plus 70s–90s pan-Mediterranean pop, with an edition at La Bellevilloise. — [CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Noise/The-SWANA-Events-Enriching-Europe-s-Music-Scene)
- Disco Dabké calls itself an "unapologetic" 90s/00s Arabic pop night with a kitsch aesthetic and has held events in Dubai, Beirut and Paris. — [CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Noise/The-SWANA-Events-Enriching-Europe-s-Music-Scene)
- Other European SWANA nights stress inclusion and safety (Bukhar, Copenhagen: "safety and inclusion for people of any gender or orientation"), activism and nightlife (Nazar, London), and female/marginalised artists (Habibti Nation). — [CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Noise/The-SWANA-Events-Enriching-Europe-s-Music-Scene)
- Mawimbi (Paris) is described as an "unconventional" mix of ghetto house, modern highlife and "shamanic techno". This is the journalist's wording, not the collective's own. — [OkayAfrica](https://okayafrica.com/mawimbi-collective-club-music-ghetto-house-modern-highlife)
- Tunisian producer Deena Abdelwahed is profiled as a reference for "SWANA electro: syncretism, commitment". Montreal's "Sweet Swana" series discusses a "fair share" for Arab electro. — [PAN M 360](https://panm360.com/en/?p=116791); [PAN M 360](https://panm360.com/en/?p=109547)
- Acid Arab (originally from Paris) is touring live, e.g. Marseille, 9 Jan 2027. — [MyProvence](https://www.myprovence.fr/agenda/concert/marseille/acid-arab-en-live)

### Inferences
- Recurring vocabulary: "SWANA" (a deliberate decolonial term in place of "Middle East"), "hafla", "story", "diaspora", "safe/inclusive", and specific eras or genres (Shaabi, Mahraganat, Raï, Gnawa, dabke, tarab). Naming exact genres and artists reads as more credible and less stereotyped than "oriental vibes" imagery (camels, lanterns, belly dance, Arabic-looking fonts).
- Tekonest's planned breadth (Levant to Morocco plus Afro plus electronic) is close to Radio Flouka's positioning. Partnering with such collectives may work better than competing with them head-on.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the visual identity (logos, colours, typography) of Radio Flouka, Beirut Electro Parade or others. I found no URLs for most of their own sites.
- The CairoScene article is dated Feb 2023 and its event dates are outdated. Current 2026 activity of each collective was not checked except Radio Flouka and Acid Arab.
- Not researched: Paris press (Télérama, Les Inrocks, Trax) on the SWANA scene. Time Out Paris has a "collectifs soirées" list ([Time Out](https://www.timeout.fr/paris/concert-soiree/collectifs-soirees)) that I did not fetch.

## Q3. What artists/technicians expect from a network, and what organisers expect when seeking crew

### Takeaway
Pay is the main pain point for artists (40% of DJs report lower pay than before the pandemic). The most concrete fair pay model found publishes fixed fee floors and a career-phase calculator. Crew platforms that work (Crew United) are built on verified credits, clear entry criteria and job offers that must meet collective-agreement budgets.

### Cited Findings
- IMS Business Report 2024: 40% of DJs say they are "generally being paid less" than before the pandemic, and another 40% struggle to find gigs, even though the industry grew 17% in 2023. — [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/ims-business-report-2024-reveals-that-tech-house-still-leads-as-the-most-popular-electronic-genre-news)
- The Dutch Fair Pop Pilot (fairPACCT / Platform ACCT, research by Erasmus University Rotterdam, report March 2025) set gross fees per musician per show of €284 (Phase 1) and €456.50 (Phase 2). Acts are placed in a phase by an 11-criteria "Fair Pay Rekentool", and travel and technical costs are reimbursed on top. It covered 151 shows at 8 venues, Sep–Nov 2024. Its recommendations stress clear communication, clear eligibility criteria and data verification. — [VNPF PDF](https://vnpf.nl/content/uploads/2025/03/fairPACCT-Fair-Pop-Pilot_FINAAL.pdf)
- Crew United (European film/TV crew network) lets only professionals with practical experience register. Registrations are checked against commercial credits and training. Profiles show a verified filmography and who worked with whom. Productions can post jobs for free, and castings are forwarded only if "the budget fits collective agreements". Advanced search, follow and Cast & Crew Manager require paid tiers. — [Crew United FAQ](https://www.crew-united.com/en/croft/render/home/faq)
- MEAA (Australia) campaigns for the industry to "pay up" and stop exploiting professional musicians. This is evidence that fair pay is a live advocacy theme. — [MEAA](https://www.meaa.org/mediaroom/time-for-9-billion-industry-to-pay-up-and-stop-exploiting-professional-musicians/)

### Inferences
- For artists/technicians, a credible "join" flow likely needs: published minimum rates or a rate grid (in the Fair Pop style), stated selection criteria, a credits/portfolio profile, and a clear rule on who pays and when. A vague "fairly paid" claim without numbers may read as marketing.
- For organisers, Crew United suggests they value searchable verified credits, a quick free way to post a need, and assurance of legal or collective-agreement compliance. In France this means intermittent du spectacle status and GUSO/contracts, which I did not verify in this session.
- Since branch 3 (services) is only "proposed", the organiser path on the home page could start as a simple "Tell us about your event" contact form.

### Gaps
- No French-specific source checked on technicians' expectations (intermittence, CCN des entreprises techniques au service de la création et de l'événement, minimum wage grids), nor on French platforms such as Shotgun pro features or "Côté Régie". This needs separate research.
- No survey found on what event organisers want from crew/production providers. That point is inferred from Crew United's design.

## Q4. Best practices for multi-audience home pages (audience routing, segmented CTAs)

### Takeaway
NN/g warns that audience-based navigation "will often degrade usability" because people fall into several groups and feel they must check every segment. Audience routing should therefore be a light layer on the home page (segmented CTAs or entry cards). The main navigation should be organised by tasks/content.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g: "Segmenting a website's navigation by audience categories will often degrade usability." The risks are overlapping users who fit several categories, and users who browse several segments to avoid missing content. — [NN/g video page](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/audience-based-website-navigation/)
- University of St Andrews' digital team argues against breaking the user experience with audience-segmented sites, citing NN/g. — [St Andrews digital comms](https://digitalcommunications.wp.st-andrews.ac.uk/2014/08/21/dont-break-the-user-experience/) (title from search only; not fetched)
- Slickplan argues that navigation should reflect users' language and tasks, not internal org structure ("website navigation is for your users not your C-suite"). — [Slickplan](https://slickplan.com/blog/website-navigation-is-for-your-users-not-your-c-suite) (not fetched; title only)

### Inferences
- For Tekonest: put three clear entry cards or CTAs in the hero area ("Sortir / Next nights", "Rejoindre le réseau (artistes & techniciens)", "Organiser un événement / Nous contacter"). Below them, place a task-based main nav: Events, Network, Services, Learn (BTS Son + TekPractice), About. Mark branch status honestly ("en préparation", "bientôt").
- A student who is an event-goer and a technician will fit several audiences. Card labels should therefore describe actions ("Find a night", "Join the network"), not identities ("I'm an artist").

### Gaps
- The NN/g homepage guidelines article and gov design system pages (GOV.UK, DSFR) could not be fetched or were not reached. No Baymard source was checked. The nav labels above are untested proposals.

## Q5. Brand architecture theory for a small founder-led umbrella

### Takeaway
Aaker & Joachimsthaler's Brand Relationship Spectrum makes the branded house the default. A separate brand is justified only when it is "absolutely necessary" (a new association to own, a truly different offering, avoiding a damaging association, channel conflict) and the business can fund it. A one-person umbrella should use one master brand with descriptive branch names.

### Cited Findings
- The spectrum has four strategies and nine sub-strategies: house of brands (not connected, shadow endorser), endorsed brands (token endorsement, linked name, strong endorsement), sub-brands (co-drivers, master brand as driver), and branded house (different identity, same identity). — [Aaker & Joachimsthaler 2000, CMR (PDF via Nova SBE)](https://moodle.novasbe.pt/pluginfile.php/672530/mod_resource/content/1/aaker-joachimsthaler-2000-the-brand-relationship-spectrum-the-key-to-the-brand-architecture-challenge.pdf)
- Move toward a branded house when the master brand adds associations, credibility, visibility or communication efficiency, and when the new offering strengthens it. Separate brands require a compelling need, and the business must be large and durable enough to fund the new brand. — [same PDF](https://moodle.novasbe.pt/pluginfile.php/672530/mod_resource/content/1/aaker-joachimsthaler-2000-the-brand-relationship-spectrum-the-key-to-the-brand-architecture-challenge.pdf)
- The paper warns for co-driver sub-brands that a weaker brand can tarnish a stronger one if quality differs. — [same PDF](https://moodle.novasbe.pt/pluginfile.php/672530/mod_resource/content/1/aaker-joachimsthaler-2000-the-brand-relationship-spectrum-the-key-to-the-brand-architecture-challenge.pdf)
- Further reading (not fetched): WARC "Branded House and House of Brands" toolkit, and Branding Strategy Insider on brand architecture and the "one brand strategy". — [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/article/warc-exclusive/the-brand-strategy-toolkit-branded-house-and-house-of-brands/en-gb/135102); [BSI](https://brandingstrategyinsider.com/brand-architecture-for-competitive-advantage/)

### Inferences
- Tekonest: branded house with "master brand as driver" for Nights, Network, Services and Learn. TekPractice could be the one endorsed product ("TekPractice, by Tekonest"), because it may serve users who never attend a night (BTS students nationwide).
- Possible tension: a nightlife brand and a free exam-prep platform reach different audiences. If parents, schools or teachers would hesitate at club associations, that matches Aaker's "avoid a damaging association" criterion. It argues for endorsing the learning branch rather than fully merging it. This is my judgement, not a sourced finding.

### Gaps
- No empirical study found on brand architecture for very small or creative collectives specifically. The theory comes from corporate brand literature.
