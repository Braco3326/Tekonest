# Build Tekonest as one honest branded house

> **Note, 24 Oct 2026.** This is a dated record. Its cultural-mix framing (mixed-culture nights, Arab/Afro/electronic programming) is superseded: that idea belongs to the separate project [Rebels × Tekonest](../../projects/rebels-x-tekonest/README.md), not to Tekonest's identity (brand decision D-08). Tekonest is a Paris umbrella for the craft of sound and live production. Kept unchanged below as the record of what was studied.


Tekonest should be one master brand with descriptive branch names (Nuits, Réseau, Services, Apprendre), plus one endorsed product, "TekPractice, by Tekonest". It needs a fixed visual core (wordmark, one neutral bilingual-ready type family, colour tokens) and a flexible accent layer for each programme. It should be anchored in real Paris places and real people, not in "oriental" or generic club codes. The comparable organisations all put one name over events, education and media. The one well-known education-plus-events platform, Red Bull Music Academy, closed when its agency partnership ended. That supports a cheap, self-sufficient learning branch under the main name, not a separately funded sub-brand. On the home page, the evidence argues against audience-based main navigation. Three action-labelled entry cards in the hero ("Trouver une soirée", "Rejoindre le réseau", "Organiser un événement") should sit above a task-based menu, and every branch should carry a visible status label. On type, IBM Plex is the strongest open-licence fit: Plex Sans, Plex Sans Arabic and Plex Mono are all SIL OFL, designed as one system, and can be self-hosted. Satoshi and General Sans are not open-licence. The legal side shapes the creative side. In France a recognisable person's image is personal data that needs written, use-specific consent, and stock "people" photos cannot honestly stand in for Tekonest's community. So the imagery should be a small set of consented, credited portraits, and the copy should avoid the point médian, which screen readers handle badly. Tekonest is probably exempt from the European Accessibility Act as a micro-structure, but WCAG 2.2 AA is still the right target.

## One master brand fits a one-founder umbrella

Brand architecture theory points to a branded house. Aaker and Joachimsthaler's Brand Relationship Spectrum runs from house of brands through endorsed brands and sub-brands to branded house. It treats separate brands as justified only when there is a compelling need, such as a new association to own, a genuinely different offering, or a damaging association to avoid, **and** when the business is large and durable enough to fund them ([Aaker & Joachimsthaler 2000](https://moodle.novasbe.pt/pluginfile.php/672530/mod_resource/content/1/aaker-joachimsthaler-2000-the-brand-relationship-spectrum-the-key-to-the-brand-architecture-challenge.pdf)). The same paper warns that a weaker co-driver sub-brand can tarnish a stronger one when quality differs ([Aaker & Joachimsthaler 2000](https://moodle.novasbe.pt/pluginfile.php/672530/mod_resource/content/1/aaker-joachimsthaler-2000-the-brand-relationship-spectrum-the-key-to-the-brand-architecture-challenge.pdf)). That warning matters for a project where three of five branches are still concepts or proposals.

The comparables show the same pattern. **Boiler Room** keeps streaming, ticketed events, merchandise and brand partnerships under one name. It passed through DICE ownership and was sold to Superstruct Entertainment in January 2025 ([DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/boiler-room-acquired-festival-giant-superstruct-entertainment)). In Paris, **Collectif MU** ran Garage MU for about 15 years before leading La Station – Gare des Mines, a "laboratoire convivial et festif" combining concerts, parties, exhibitions and artist residencies ([Paris.fr](https://www.paris.fr/pages/la-station-nouveau-lieu-alternatif-et-multiculturel-parisien-3799)). It funds part of that with Île-de-France residency money alongside the club activity ([Île-de-France](https://www.iledefrance.fr/tous-les-services/ma-region-pres-de-chez-moi/culture/arts-plastiques-numeriques-et-urbains/212381-residences-artistiques-et-ateliers-la-station-gare-des-mines)). Mawimbi works as both party collective and "nascent label" under one name ([OkayAfrica](https://okayafrica.com/mawimbi-collective-club-music-ghetto-house-modern-highlife)). Berlin's Sahra and London's Majazz Project pair nights with DJ workshops or archival research ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Noise/The-SWANA-Events-Enriching-Europe-s-Music-Scene)). In each case the night is the driver and education is an extension. The cautionary case is **Red Bull Music Academy**. Its lectures, workshops and concerts were run by the agency Yadastar from 1998, and they were shut down when that partnership ended and Red Bull moved away from "a strongly centralized approach" ([RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/say-goodbye-to-red-bull-music-academy-and-radio/)). Learning that depends on one partner can disappear.

For Tekonest, the master brand should drive Nuits, Réseau, Services and Apprendre as plain descriptors set in the same typeface. TekPractice is the one exception worth endorsing rather than absorbing. It already has a coined name, and it may serve BTS Son students nationwide who will never attend a night. Parents or schools may also hesitate at a club association, which is Aaker's "avoid a damaging association" criterion. That last point is a judgement, not a sourced finding. Even so, the safe structure is "TekPractice, by Tekonest": the product can travel without the brand, and the brand still gets credit for it.

## Cultural specificity beats ornament in a crowded Paris scene

Tekonest is not entering an empty field. **Radio Flouka** already calls itself a cultural, political and musical hub telling "the MENA story" from Paris ([Black Rhino Radio](https://blackrhinoradio.com/interviews/radio-flouka-parisian-echoes-of-the-mena)), runs open airs at Le Hasard Ludique listed on the City of Paris agenda ([Paris.fr](https://www.paris.fr/evenements/open-air-radio-flouka-x-corps-libre-67733)), and has played Nuits Sonores ([Nuits Sonores](https://nuits-sonores.com/en/artiste/radio-flouka-2/)). Beirut Electro Parade grew out of founder Hadi Zeidan's homesickness for Beirut's scene. Disco Dabké sells an "unapologetic" kitsch 90s/00s Arabic pop night. Copenhagen's Bukhar leads with "safety and inclusion for people of any gender or orientation" ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Noise/The-SWANA-Events-Enriching-Europe-s-Music-Scene)). These collectives describe themselves through a specific story, era or genre and an explicit mission, not through generic regional imagery. Tekonest's Maghreb-to-Levant-plus-Afro-plus-electronic breadth overlaps heavily with Radio Flouka. Partnering with such collectives is likely to be more productive than competing with them. A real point of difference would be the technician and fair-pay focus, which none of these collectives leads with.

Typography should follow the Khatt Foundation's principle of **bilingual matchmaking**. In *Typographic Matchmaking*, five Dutch–Arab designer pairs, including Gerard Unger with Nadine Chahine and Peter Bilak with Tarek Atrissi, co-developed Arabic companions to Latin faces so that Arabic met "international quality standards" instead of being decorative ([Typotheque](https://www.typotheque.com/blog/new-book-typographic-matchmaking)). Khatt later extended the method to the Maghrib ([Khatt](https://www.khtt.net/en/page/1315/typographic-matchmaking-in-the-maghrib-3.0)). Its symposia explicitly aim at "going beyond the stereotypes of what is associated with Arabic culture" ([Khatt](https://www.khtt.net/en/page/2092/the-typographic-landscapes-of-the-arab-world)). The type trade itself catalogues Latin "imitation" fonts made to look Semitic ([MyFonts](https://www.myfonts.com/es/pages/fontshop-fontlists-faux-hebrew-fonts/fontshop-fontlists-deborahs-semitic-imitation-fonts)), and that faux-script category is exactly what to avoid. In practice, Tekonest should show cultural presence through real Arabic text, such as artist names and programme titles correctly set right-to-left and proofed by native readers, and through named genres and artists and photographs of real people. It should not use pseudo-Arabic lettering, arabesques, crescents or lanterns. The research found no primary written pitfall list or documented Afro-diasporic identity case study, so this guidance rests on the Khatt framing rather than a checklist.

The best current French model for an identity that changes while staying recognisable is **Nuits Sonores**. Its 2025 identity by DIA Studio sets an expressive particle layer over a neutral custom grotesque that works as "a visual metronome tying the identity to one beat", with a single grainy neon-green accent the audience adopted as its own ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/dia-studio-nuits-sonores-graphic-design-project-181125)). DIA handed the organiser a generative tool so the system kept working after the project ended ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/dia-studio-nuits-sonores-graphic-design-project-181125)). The 2024 identity, by Super Terrain, anchored itself in the Grandes Locos venue, a former SNCF railway workshop ([Fonts In Use](https://www.fontsinuse.com/uses/57326/nuits-sonores-2024)). In 2026 the anchors were the Rhône, the Saône and the same industrial site ([Nuits Sonores](https://nuits-sonores.com/en/visual-identity-2026/)). Scaled down for Tekonest, this means a fixed wordmark, one neutral family and colour tokens, with one accent per branch and a flexible photographic or generative layer rooted in Paris places. That accent can bridge the existing dark learning site and the white TekPractice simulator.

## IBM Plex gives a French-first, Arabic-ready voice under an open licence

All eleven Google Fonts/Fontsource candidates checked are **SIL OFL 1.1** and include the `latin-ext` subset needed for French diacritics. Only four ship an Arabic subset: IBM Plex Sans Arabic, Noto Sans Arabic, Readex Pro and Rubik ([npm @fontsource/ibm-plex-sans-arabic](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@fontsource/ibm-plex-sans-arabic); [npm @fontsource/readex-pro](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@fontsource/readex-pro); [npm @fontsource/rubik](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@fontsource/rubik); [npm @fontsource/noto-sans-arabic](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@fontsource/noto-sans-arabic)). Satoshi and General Sans, both popular in startup branding, are Fontshare fonts that "are not open-source and are still controlled by an ITF EULA" ([Indian Type Foundry](https://www.indiantypefoundry.com/news/introducing-fontshare)). Neither is on Fontsource, and their self-hosting terms could not be verified.

| Option | Role | Arabic | Variable | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **IBM Plex Sans + Plex Sans Arabic + Plex Mono** | Whole system: brand, UI, technical/learning layer | Yes, as a designed companion | Sans yes; Arabic and Mono static | More font files, but one owner and one design system |
| Readex Pro | Single-file Latin + Arabic | Yes | Yes (wght, HEXP) | Geometric "tech" tone, no italics |
| Bricolage Grotesque / Instrument Sans / Fraunces | Display personality (Latin only) | No; pair with Noto Sans Arabic | Yes | Two-family system to maintain |
| Inter | UI fallback | No | Yes | Too generic to carry a recognisable brand |

Sources for the table: [npm @fontsource/inter](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@fontsource/inter), [npm @fontsource/readex-pro](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@fontsource/readex-pro), [npm @fontsource/ibm-plex-sans-arabic](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@fontsource/ibm-plex-sans-arabic).

Plex is the recommendation because it delivers both the matchmaking principle and a "studio-grade" monospace for the BTS Son and TekPractice layer from a single family. If Tekonest wants more character in headlines, the best option is a Bricolage Grotesque display layer over Plex text. Fonts should be self-hosted as WOFF2. The OFL permits commercial web use with no visible credit as long as the full font package, including the licence, is available, and it recommends shipping the full OFL text ([OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/)). Converting or subsetting normally counts as modification, so a font with a Reserved Font Name should be renamed in CSS unless functional equivalence is kept ([OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/)). Self-hosting also avoids the issue in LG München I's 2022 ruling. That court found that loading Google Fonts from Google's servers passed visitors' IP addresses to Google without consent and awarded **€100 damages** ([IHK Bergisches Land](https://www.ihk.de/bergische/recht-und-steuern/wettbewerbsrecht/google-fonts-5646176)). The ruling is German, not French, but the GDPR reasoning applies across the EU.

The logo should be built as a small system, not a single file. eBay's published playbook is a useful benchmark. It defines clear space from the logo's own x-height, sets a **24 px minimum on-screen height**, specifies a white-on-dark version for small containers, and uses a **separate simplified symbol for the favicon** that works in both light and dark browser modes ([eBay Playbook](https://playbook.ebay.com/foundations/logo)). Tekonest therefore needs an outlined SVG wordmark, a compact monogram drawn on a pixel grid for 16 px and 32 px, and black, white and accent single-colour versions, all tested on both the dark learning site and the white simulator. Michael Bierut's view that a logo's meaning "happens in our own mind" through use ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/michael-bierut-logos-indaba)) argues for a simple mark applied consistently, not a mark that tries to depict every branch.

## Consent law turns imagery into a portrait practice

French law and honest branding point the same way. Service-Public states that "votre image est une donnée personnelle". Publishing a recognisable person requires **written consent** that names the occasion and covers one specific use. Being photographed is not consent to online publication, and people who appear "de manière isolée et reconnaissable" in a public place must also agree ([Service-Public.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F32103)). Minors need written parental authorisation with "pas d'exceptions possibles" ([Service-Public mirror](https://sites.service-information-publique.fr/vias/guide-associations/F32103.html)). The exception for a "manifestation publique" applies only where nobody is singled out and there is no commercial use ([Service-Public.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F32103)). The sources disagree on the criminal penalty for images taken in a private place: the current page cites **one year and €15,000** ([Service-Public.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F32103)), while a 2016 mirror cites €45,000 ([Service-Public mirror](https://sites.service-information-publique.fr/vias/guide-associations/F32103.html)). Either way, the risk is serious. The Paris 2024 adult image-and-voice release is a usable template for Tekonest's own form ([Académie de Bordeaux](https://ent2d.ac-bordeaux.fr/disciplines/eps/wp-content/uploads/sites/33/2024/03/Paris-2024-Autorisation-captation-utilisation-image-voix-majeur.pdf)).

Free stock does not solve the problem. Unsplash grants a broad commercial licence but "cannot make any guarantees" about model releases and tells users to check with contributors ([Unsplash Help](https://help.unsplash.com/en/articles/2612329-releases-and-trademark)). Pexels forbids implying "endorsement of your product by people or brands on the imagery" ([Pexels License](https://www.pexels.com/license/)). Showing stock faces as "nos artistes" or "nos techniciens" is therefore misleading in principle and risky under the licence terms. The creative direction follows from this. Until real photos exist, Tekonest should use equipment, scene or abstract imagery, or placeholders visibly labelled "Image d'illustration". It should then build a growing series of authored, consented and credited portraits ("© Nom / Tekonest") of artists, technicians at work and learners, in preference to anonymous crowd shots. A simple asset register (source, licence, date, release status) for every image makes this manageable. Signage at the venue entrance and a visible "no photo" marker are good practice, but they do not replace individual consent for isolated, recognisable people.

## WCAG 2.2 AA and épicène French set the floor for every page

Tekonest is most likely outside the legal scope of accessibility rules. The European Accessibility Act has applied in France since **28 June 2025**, but it exempts service providers with fewer than 10 employees and under €2 million turnover ([DGCCRF](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/dgccrf/les-fiches-pratiques/professionnels-vos-produits-et-services-doivent-etre-conformes-la-directive-accessibilite)). The RGAA's original scope covers public bodies and large companies ([Décret 2019-768](https://www.cfdt-ufetam.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/decret_2019-768_24-07-19_accessibilite_handicapes_services_com_en_ligne.pdf)). Whether an association counts as a "prestataire" under the EAA was not confirmed. Even so, a brand that claims inclusion should still meet **WCAG 2.2 AA** ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)), and a short "Déclaration d'accessibilité" page is a cheap signal of that commitment.

The criteria that most affect the design system are these. Text contrast must be at least **4.5:1**, or 3:1 for text of at least 24 px (18.66 px bold). Input borders, icons and focus rings need **3:1** against adjacent colours. Layouts must reflow at **320 CSS px** and survive user text-spacing overrides. Auto-moving content lasting over five seconds needs a pause control. Nothing may flash more than three times per second. Every drag gesture needs a single-pointer alternative. Focused elements must not be entirely hidden behind a sticky header or cookie banner ([W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)). Targets must be at least **24 × 24 CSS px** or adequately spaced ([W3C Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). Repeated help or contact links must stay in the same relative order on every page ([W3C Understanding 3.2.6](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/consistent-help.html)). For a nightlife brand that wants neon accents and motion, this sets the accent colour's usable range. Status badges such as "En préparation" are text and need 4.5:1, and should never rely on colour alone. Particle or generative layers in the Nuits Sonores style need a pause control and should honour `prefers-reduced-motion`.

French copy should be inclusive without the point médian. In 2017 the Fédération des aveugles de France called écriture inclusive "proprement indéchiffrable par nos lecteurs d'écrans" ([Handicap.fr](https://informations.handicap.fr/a-ecriture-inclusive-accessible-10358.php)). The FFDys president disagreed that it is a major barrier for dyslexic readers ([Handicap.fr](https://informations.handicap.fr/a-ecriture-inclusive-accessible-10358.php)). The 21 November 2017 circular, which binds only the State administration, discourages the point médian but requires feminised job titles and recommends doublets ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000036068906)). The working rule for Tekonest is épicène words first ("les artistes", "l'équipe technique", "le public"), direct address with "vous", a full doublet when a gendered noun is unavoidable ("techniciennes et techniciens"), feminised titles for named women ("la régisseuse"), and never "·", "(ne)" or slashes in buttons and headings.

Typographic polish also signals local credibility. Put a narrow no-break space before ; : ! ? and inside « », and use only French guillemets ([Debian](https://www.debian.org/international/french/typographie)). Accent capitals, including "À", as the Académie française asks ([Académie française](https://dictionnaire-academie.fr/article/QDL005)). Write "100 %" and decimal commas ([Blog du Modérateur](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/regles-ponctuation-typographie/)). Set `<html lang="fr">` and tag English fragments with `lang="en"`. Write source text in lowercase with accents, so that CSS uppercase keeps the "É".

## Route by action in the hero, organise by task underneath

Nielsen Norman Group warns that "segmenting a website's navigation by audience categories will often degrade usability". People belong to several groups and end up checking every segment for fear of missing something ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/audience-based-website-navigation/)). Tekonest's audiences overlap by design: a BTS Son student can be an event-goer, a junior technician and a learner on the same evening. The home page should therefore route through **three action-labelled cards in the hero**, not identity labels such as "Je suis artiste":

| Card | Primary CTA | Destination | Honest status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sortir | « Trouver une soirée » | Nuits: upcoming dates, or newsletter sign-up | « Premières soirées en préparation » |
| Rejoindre | « Rejoindre le réseau » | Réseau: criteria, rates, profile | « Réseau en construction » |
| Organiser | « Parlez-nous de votre événement » | Services: simple contact | « Offre en réflexion » |

Below the cards, the main navigation should be organised by task: Soirées, Réseau, Services, Apprendre (BTS Son + TekPractice), À propos, with Contact in a fixed position. Apprendre can stay a quieter secondary route, because its audience usually arrives by search or from school, not from nightlife.

Each route has to earn trust in its own way. For artists and technicians, the problem to address is pay. The IMS Business Report 2024 found that **40% of DJs** say they are paid less than before the pandemic, even though the industry grew 17% in 2023 ([Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/ims-business-report-2024-reveals-that-tech-house-still-leads-as-the-most-popular-electronic-genre-news)). The most concrete fair-pay model found, the Dutch Fair Pop Pilot, published gross fee floors of **€284 and €456.50 per musician per show**, assigned each act a career phase through an 11-criteria calculator, reimbursed travel and technical costs on top, and recommended clear eligibility criteria and data verification ([VNPF fairPACCT report](https://vnpf.nl/content/uploads/2025/03/fairPACCT-Fair-Pop-Pilot_FINAAL.pdf)). A "Rejoindre" page that states selection criteria, a rate principle and who pays when will be more convincing than "rémunération équitable" without numbers. For organisers, Crew United shows what works on a crew platform: registration verified against professional credits, free job posting, and job offers forwarded only if "the budget fits collective agreements" ([Crew United FAQ](https://www.crew-united.com/en/croft/render/home/faq)). While Services is only proposed, the organiser card should lead to a short "Parlez-nous de votre événement" form, and only once that form actually works. Until then, follow the GDPR logic and do not collect data that cannot be processed. Show a disabled button with visible text such as "Formulaire en préparation — écrivez-nous à …", and never a fake "Merci, c'est envoyé !". The CNIL's own model-form pages could not be retrieved, so the exact notice wording needs checking before any form goes live ([CNIL](https://cnil.fr/fr/exemples-de-formulaire-de-collecte-de-donnees-caractere-personnel)).

## Conclusion

Tekonest's main strategic risk is not visual blandness but over-promising: five branches, three of them unbuilt, and one name over them all. The research suggests the same remedy at every layer. Status labels, published pay criteria, consented portraits instead of stock faces, a disabled form instead of a fake confirmation, and endorsement for TekPractice rather than full fusion all make the brand look more established, not less. A small brand that is visibly honest about what exists can build credibility faster than a polished brand whose services do not yet exist.

The second implication is that Tekonest's most defensible position may be the technician and fair-pay angle, not the cultural mix. Paris already has strong SWANA and Afro-electronic collectives with clear stories. Few of them, if any, put fair pay for the backline, the stage crew and the BTS Son pipeline at the centre. That is the position where the network, the learning platform and TekPractice reinforce each other. Two questions remain open and need dedicated research before launch: French technician pay grids under intermittence and the relevant collective agreements, and confirmation of whether an association falls under the EAA.
