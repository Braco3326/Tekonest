# Visual references, typography and logo practice for Tekonest (umbrella cultural/creative brand, Paris)

Research date: 2026-10-22. Method note: about 20 tool calls. Font licence and subset data were checked directly against the npm registry packages published by Fontsource (tarball `metadata.json` and file listings). The Fontsource web API and jsDelivr returned 403 through the proxy, so the registry was used instead. Anything not checked against a primary page is marked **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## Q1. Current (2023–2026) music/culture identity work: minimalist identities with personality, and how they handle sub-programmes

### Takeaway
The best-documented recent French case is Nuits Sonores (Lyon). Its identity is rebuilt every year by a different designer around a single strong idea: a typographic or particle system plus one accent colour. Continuity comes from the festival name and its framework, not from a fixed look. The 2025 DIA Studio system pairs a neutral custom grotesque "metronome" with a generative motion layer, and the client received a tool so the system keeps working after handover. That model fits an umbrella brand that has several programmes.

### Cited Findings
- **Nuits Sonores 2025, by DIA Studio (Mitch Paone).** The concept is "recreating the pulse of music and rhythm through typography and particles", with particles blasting out of the letterforms. Paone calls the result a "techno meets punk vibe". — [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/dia-studio-nuits-sonores-graphic-design-project-181125)
- **Type: neutral anchor plus expressive layer.** The expressive layer sits on top of MNKY Klaus, a custom typeface from MNKY Type. The neutral face acts as "a visual metronome tying the identity to one beat." — [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/dia-studio-nuits-sonores-graphic-design-project-181125)
- **Colour: a single accent.** The accent is a grainy neon green. The audience adopted it, wearing the colour and dyeing their hair to match. — [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/dia-studio-nuits-sonores-graphic-design-project-181125)
- **System and handover.** DIA built a generative tool with creative technologist André Burnier. It produces "extremely high-resolution stills and motion in different aspect ratios" across web, social, signage, posters and merchandise. The organiser, Arty Farty, received a custom tool to keep using the system after the project ended. The article does **not** describe how sub-events are differentiated. — [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/dia-studio-nuits-sonores-graphic-design-project-181125)
- **Nuits Sonores 2024, by Super Terrain** (a Nantes/Marseille collective). It used the typeface Kreuz (Emmanuel Besse, Production Type), mostly in Extended width. Two oversized metallic "NS" letters sit on a fluorescent purple-to-orange-to-blue background, and the industrial type references the Grandes Locos venue, a former SNCF railway workshop. — [Fonts In Use](https://www.fontsinuse.com/uses/57326/nuits-sonores-2024)
- **Nuits Sonores 2026, by Leeza Pritychenko** (Amsterdam). It is the festival's first three-dimensional poster: "transparent fluidity" evoking the Rhône and Saône rivers, metallic structures referencing the Grandes Locos, and organic forms reclaiming an industrial wasteland. The page says nothing about type or colour. — [Nuits Sonores](https://nuits-sonores.com/en/visual-identity-2026/)
- **Pattern across the three years.** The organiser changes designer every year and anchors each identity in **place** (the venue, the rivers) rather than in genre clichés. — synthesis of the [Fonts In Use](https://www.fontsinuse.com/uses/57326/nuits-sonores-2024) and [Nuits Sonores 2026](https://nuits-sonores.com/en/visual-identity-2026/) pages

### Inferences
- **A fixed core with a flexible layer suits Tekonest.** The core is the logotype, one neutral grotesque and the colour tokens. The flexible layer is a per-programme accent or a generative or photographic layer. This is the Nuits Sonores logic, scaled down.
- **Anchor identity in real places and the people involved.** Nuits Sonores roots its look in Lyon venues and rivers. Tekonest can do the same with Paris places, its artists and its technicians instead of reaching for "oriental" or "nightclub" codes.
- **Sub-programme handling (my recommendation, not taken from a source).** Keep one wordmark and add a descriptor in the same typeface (for example "Tekonest Nuits", "Tekonest Réseau", "TekPractice"). Use one accent colour per programme, and keep the dark and light modes aligned with the dark learning site and the white simulator.

### Gaps
- **Not researched (tool-call budget):** Sónar, CTM, Villette Sonique, Musiques Sacrées de Fès, the Institut du monde arabe identity, Pentagram/Kiosk projects, Spassky Fischer, Building Paris, Atelier Pentagon and Vier5. A search for Villette Sonique's identity returned no designer credit, and the IMA searches returned only institutional or historical pages with no identity case study.
- **Brand New / UnderConsideration:** no relevant 2023–2026 music or culture review was surfaced.
- **Sub-programme handling:** no source found that documents it explicitly for any of these festivals.

## Q2. Representing Arab/SWANA and African programming respectfully (bilingual type, Khatt, pitfalls)

### Takeaway
The main documented practice is **bilingual typographic matchmaking**: Arabic and Latin designers co-develop matching typefaces of equal quality, so Arabic is not a decorative afterthought. The Khatt Foundation frames its work as "going beyond the stereotypes of what is associated with Arabic culture". Detailed written pitfall lists (faux-Arabic Latin fonts, ornament as shorthand) were **not** retrieved from a primary source in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- ***Typographic Matchmaking* (Typotheque, 2007), initiated by the Khatt Foundation.** Five Dutch–Arab pairs adapted existing Latin typefaces into matching Arabic versions:
  - Gerard Unger and Nadine Chahine
  - Martin Majoor and Pascal Zoghbi
  - Lucas de Groot and Mounir Al Shaarani
  - Peter Bilak and Tarek Atrissi
  - Fred Smeijers and Lara Assouad Khoury

  The stated goal was fonts for the Arab world that meet "international quality standards". — [Typotheque](https://www.typotheque.com/blog/new-book-typographic-matchmaking)
- **Typographic Matchmaking in the Maghrib 3.0.** A later Khatt research project with the same method, applied to the Maghrib. — [Khatt](https://www.khtt.net/en/page/1315/typographic-matchmaking-in-the-maghrib-3.0)
- **Perception and stereotypes.** Khatt's "Kufi & Kaffiya" symposium listed among its topics "going beyond the stereotypes of what is associated with Arabic culture". Tarek Atrissi's talk addressed how Arab visual culture is perceived "within the Arab world itself" and "in the western world". — [Khatt](https://www.khtt.net/en/page/2092/the-typographic-landscapes-of-the-arab-world)
- ***Visual Narratives from Arabia*** (H. Smitshuijzen AbiFarès and T. Atrissi, UNESCO, 2007). A case study of "the typographic landscape across the Arab world". — [Khatt](https://www.khtt.net/en/page/2092/the-typographic-landscapes-of-the-arab-world)
- **Khatt Foundation background.** Wikipedia page on the Khatt Foundation (Center for Arabic Typography). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khatt_Foundation)
- **Practitioner discussion.** Type designers discuss pairing Arabic with Latin typefaces on TypeDrawers. This is forum opinion, not authoritative. — [TypeDrawers](https://typedrawers.com/discussion/comment/59684)
- **"Foreign-looking" Latin fonts exist as a genre.** The type trade itself catalogues "imitation" script fonts (Latin letters styled to look Hebrew or Semitic). This shows the faux-script category exists, which is the thing to avoid. — [MyFonts/FontShop list](https://www.myfonts.com/es/pages/fontshop-fontlists-faux-hebrew-fonts/fontshop-fontlists-deborahs-semitic-imitation-fonts)
- **Reference book.** *Arabic for Designers* (Mourad Boutros, second edition 2017), a practitioner guide to using Arabic script correctly. — [Boutros Fonts](https://boutrosfonts.com/Arabic-for-Designers-Second-Edition.html)

### Inferences
- **Choose families designed as Arabic–Latin pairs** rather than an arbitrary Arabic font placed next to a Latin one. Candidates are IBM Plex Sans + IBM Plex Sans Arabic, Readex Pro, Noto Sans + Noto Sans Arabic, and Rubik (see Q3).
- **Never use pseudo-Arabic Latin lettering, arabesque patterns or crescent/lantern motifs as shorthand.** Signal cultural presence through real Arabic text (artist names and programme titles set correctly in RTL), through the programming itself and through photographs of real people.
- **Have native readers proof all Arabic text.** Common errors are disconnected letters from broken shaping and reversed RTL order. This is common practitioner knowledge, but no source was retrieved in this pass. **[UNVERIFIED]**

### Gaps
- **Not retrieved:**
  - Eye on Design (AIGA) articles on Arab or African representation in design.
  - 29LT (29Letters) pages.
  - Any documented case on African/Afro-diasporic event identities.
- **No primary written pitfall list.** No primary source found that lists pitfalls such as faux-Arabic fonts or "orientalist" ornament. A follow-up search should target Eye on Design, Khatt essays, and Bahia Shehab's or Nadine Chahine's talks.

All 11 Google Fonts / Fontsource candidates are **SIL OFL 1.1**, and all ship a `latin-ext` subset, which covers French diacritics. Four of them ship an `arabic` subset: IBM Plex Sans Arabic, Noto Sans Arabic, Readex Pro and Rubik. **Satoshi and General Sans are not OFL.** They are Fontshare/ITF fonts under a proprietary EULA and are not on Fontsource/npm.

### Inferences
- **Best match for "French-first, Arabic-ready, one family voice": IBM Plex.** IBM Plex Sans (variable) + IBM Plex Sans Arabic + IBM Plex Mono for the "studio-grade" technical and learning layer. All three are OFL, designed as one system and published by a single owner. The trade-off is that Plex Sans Arabic and Plex Mono are static files only, so they load as more font files.
- **Alternative: Readex Pro alone.** One variable file covers Latin and Arabic. It has a more geometric, slightly "tech" character and no italics.
- **Personality options for display/headlines (Latin only).** Bricolage Grotesque has an opsz/wdth range that gives a lot of character; Instrument Sans is clean and contemporary with a width axis; Fraunces is a serif with SOFT/WONK axes for editorial warmth. Each would then need an Arabic companion, for example Noto Sans Arabic (variable, wght/wdth).
- **Inter is safe but generic.** It is widely used in UI, so on its own it is unlikely to give the "recognisable personality" the founder wants.
- **Avoid Satoshi and General Sans if the requirement is "open licence".** They are free to use commercially but proprietary, cannot be installed from Fontsource, and their redistribution terms are unverified.

## Q4. Logo best practice (scalability to a 16px favicon, SVG, clear space, minimum size, monochrome)

### Takeaway
Mature brand guidelines specify clear space as a unit derived from the logo itself, a pixel minimum for screens, light and dark or knock-out variants, and a **separate simplified mark for the favicon** rather than a shrunken wordmark. Bierut's point that logos acquire meaning through use argues for a simple mark backed by consistent application.

### Cited Findings
- **eBay Playbook (a large brand's published logo rules):**
  - Clear space equals the logo's x-height ("measured from the type's baseline to the top of flat lowercase letters").
  - Clear space and layout margin are "5% of the shortest length of the layout" (about 51px on a 1024px layout).
  - Minimum size is **24px logo height on screen** and 0.25 in in print.
  - In small containers, use "a white logo on a dark color background".
  - The **favicon is a separate mark** (the shopping-bag symbol), legible in both light and dark browser modes and for web only.

  — [eBay Playbook](https://playbook.ebay.com/foundations/logo)
- **Michael Bierut (Design Indaba film, 2015):** "A lot of what we see when we're looking at the logo isn't really happening in the logo; it happens in our own mind." Meaning accrues through use. — [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/michael-bierut-logos-indaba)
- **Responsive logo systems.** The practice of tiered logo versions (full, primary, compact, icon) for different sizes is described in [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/web-design/responsive-logo/). This is a secondary agency blog of lower authority.

### Inferences
- **Tekonest deliverables:**
  1. Wordmark in SVG, with outlined text and no embedded font dependency.
  2. Compact monogram (for example a "T" or a nest/node glyph) drawn on a pixel grid so it holds up at 16×16 and 32×32.
  3. Black, white and accent single-colour versions.
  4. Clear space defined from a logo unit, such as the cap or x-height.
  5. A screen minimum of about 24px height for the wordmark (eBay's value is a reasonable benchmark).
  6. Favicon as SVG with a `prefers-color-scheme` variant, plus a PNG/ICO fallback.
- **Test the dark and light pair together.** The dark learning site and the white TekPractice need the mark tested on both backgrounds.

### Gaps
- **NN/g:** no NN/g article on logo legibility was retrieved.
- **Pentagram:** no primary Pentagram/Bierut writing on minimum sizes was retrieved.
- **Favicon sizes:** specific favicon size requirements (16/32/180/192/512 px, SVG favicon support) were not sourced in this pass. They are standard web practice. **[UNVERIFIED here]**

## Q5. Photography direction for events without stock crowds: credits, consent, French *droit à l'image*

### Takeaway
Under French practice, a person's image is personal data. Any **recognisable person shown in isolation** needs written consent that is specific to the use, even in a public place. Minors always need written parental consent. That legal framework supports the creative direction: a small number of authored portraits of real artists, technicians and guests, all consented and credited, rather than anonymous crowd shots.

### Cited Findings
- **Image as personal data.** "Votre image est une donnée personnelle" ("your image is personal data"). A person can object to its storage or diffusion without consent whenever they are recognisable. — [Service-Public.fr F32103](https://sites.service-information-publique.fr/vias/guide-associations/F32103.html)
- **Written consent, specific to one use.**
  - Consent must be written, state when and where the photo was taken, and covers **one specific use**. Reuse for another purpose needs new consent.
  - Agreeing to be photographed is not agreeing to online publication.
  - In a public place, "l'accord des personnes apparaissant de manière isolée et reconnaissable est nécessaire" (people who appear isolated and recognisable must consent).

  — [Service-Public.fr](https://sites.service-information-publique.fr/vias/guide-associations/F32103.html)
- **Exceptions:** current events (droit à l'information), artistic creation, public figures in their official role, and historical subjects. **The page does not address crowds specifically.** — [Service-Public.fr](https://sites.service-information-publique.fr/vias/guide-associations/F32103.html)
- **Minors:** written authorisation from parents or guardians, "Il n'y a pas d'exceptions possibles" (no exceptions possible). For groups of children, written authorisation is needed from all of the parents. — [Service-Public.fr](https://sites.service-information-publique.fr/vias/guide-associations/F32103.html)
- **Sanctions:**
  - Civil: an urgent removal order (référé) and damages.
  - Criminal: photographing or transmitting someone's image in a private place without consent carries 1 year's imprisonment and a €45,000 fine.
  - CNIL: a free complaint against a site that refuses removal.

  The page was last updated **21 July 2016**, so check it against the current Service-Public/CNIL version. — [Service-Public.fr](https://sites.service-information-publique.fr/vias/guide-associations/F32103.html)
- **Model release template.** The Paris 2024 template for capturing and using an adult's image and voice is a usable reference for a consent form. — [Académie de Bordeaux (PDF)](https://ent2d.ac-bordeaux.fr/disciplines/eps/wp-content/uploads/sites/33/2024/03/Paris-2024-Autorisation-captation-utilisation-image-voix-majeur.pdf)
- **Other French guides:** [Académie de Lyon DRANE (PDF)](https://drane.ac-lyon.fr/spip/IMG/pdf/droit_a_limage.pdf) and [Moselle (PDF)](https://moselia.moselle.fr/images/com_dlpb/boiteaoutils/Droit_a_limage.pdf). Both are secondary; not read in full.

### Inferences
- **Operational direction for Tekonest:**
  1. A one-page bilingual (FR/EN) release form for artists, technicians and featured guests, covering web, social and print with a duration stated.
  2. Signage at the entrance saying photography is in progress, plus a visible "no photo" marker such as a wristband or sticker. This is common practice; the legal sufficiency of signage alone for isolated, recognisable people is doubtful under the rule above. **[UNVERIFIED legal advice]**
  3. Credit every photographer ("© Name / Tekonest").
  4. Prefer portrait series of the network (artists, technicians at work, learners) over wide crowd shots. This reinforces "diversity through real people" and simplifies consent.
- **Licensing of commissioned photos.** Commissioned photographers keep authors' rights (droit d'auteur) in France, so the usage licence should be agreed in writing. **[UNVERIFIED, not researched]**

### Gaps
- **No primary sources found for:**
  - CNIL guidance specific to event photography.
  - Case law on crowd shots at concerts.
  - Documented "anti-stock" photo direction from cultural institutions.
- **Service-Public page date:** the retrieved Service-Public mirror is dated 2016, so its currency is unconfirmed.
