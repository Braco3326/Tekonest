## Techonest in five minutes

**Status: an idea, not a structure.** Techonest has no legal form, no money, no signed partner and no event booked. The three of us have agreed to co-found it; this brief prepares the decisions on how to build it.

**What it is.** A Paris collective that creates events mixing world cultures — Arab music from the Middle East to Morocco, Afro, electronic and more — and that gives young technicians, DJs and musicians paid, real-world work.

**Why now.** We are three people with complementary skills: event organising and audiovisual, house DJing with an existing Paris audience (Rebels), and Middle Eastern singing and percussion. Access to school equipment this year lowers the cost of a first test.

**What the first version would do.** One co-produced test night with Rebels, everyone paid legally, run as a non-profit association. One event, a capped budget, a clear go / stop rule. Not three events a month — not yet.

**The numbers, honestly.** With a free venue (bar deal) and three paid people, a 15 € ticket breaks even at about 155 tickets. With venue hire and five paid people it needs about 260–300. A half-full night can lose more than 1 000 €. All figures are estimates until we have real quotes (details in the working plan).

**Three decisions for this meeting**

1. What role and how much time will each of us commit?
2. Do we start as an association, and who sits on its board?
3. How do Techonest and Rebels relate: partners on one test event, or something bigger? Who decides for Rebels?

**One rule we must not break.** On paid nights, nobody works for free and nobody is paid « a percentage » off the books. French law treats that as undeclared work.

## What Techonest is, and the problem it addresses

Techonest would be a community and event label built on one belief: diverse cultures on the same stage create something richer than any single scene.

The problem it aims at has two sides. These are hypotheses from our own experience, not measured facts:

- **For audiences:** Paris nights rarely put Arab, Afro and electronic music side by side in one programme.
- **For young technicians and artists:** students and early-career people in sound, video, lighting, editing, marketing, DJing and music struggle to get paid, real event work and references.

Techonest's proposed answer: themed events through the year (Halloween and others) that hire this community, plus — later — coordinating technicians for other organisers.

## Working with Rebels: an idea, not an agreement

**Nothing has been agreed with Rebels.** This section describes what a collaboration could look like so we can discuss it; it is not a proposal anyone has accepted.

What is public: Rebels Collectif is a Paris house and Afro-house DJ collective and label presented by Play House and GrooveBox, selling tickets through Shotgun ([rebelscollectif.com](https://rebelscollectif.com/), checked 20 Oct 2026). Its listed past events include nights at Le Klub, L'Impasse, Titi Palacio and La Récolte Citadine in 2025 ([Resident Advisor](https://ra.co/promoters/159396)). I found no registered legal entity behind the name.

**Possible ways to work together (for discussion)**

| Model | Who organises and carries the risk | What Techonest brings | What Rebels brings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Rebels produces, Techonest contributes | Rebels | Cultural programme, live music, technical crew | Brand, audience, DJs, ticketing |
| 2. Techonest produces, Rebels plays | Techonest | Organisation, venue, crew, programme | DJ sets, audience reach |
| 3. True co-production | Both, by written agreement | As agreed | As agreed |

**A point to name openly.** Gabriel is part of Rebels and is also a co-founder of Techonest. That can be a strength, but every deal between the two would be on both sides of the table. We should agree how that is handled before any money moves — and Play House, as the other face of Rebels, may need a say.

## Why now, and what the first version would do

**Why it may be worth trying this season**

- The three of us cover programming, music, DJing, audiovisual and organisation between us.
- Rebels already runs nights and sells tickets, so we may not start from zero audience.
- School equipment and fellow students could lower costs this year — if the school agrees in writing.
- A single test night is cheap to learn from compared with committing to a monthly series.

**The first realistic version**

One test night, co-produced with Rebels, in a club or hall of about 150–250 people, between January and March 2027. A capped budget of about 1 000 € at risk, everyone on the night paid legally, and a written go / stop decision afterwards.

**What it will not do yet:** monthly events, a technician agency for other organisers, equipment purchases, or anyone working unpaid on a paid night.

## Decisions we need to make together

1. **Commitment.** How many hours a week can each co-founder give until March 2027, and who owns which tasks?
2. **Structure.** Start as an association (recommended), wait, or something else? Who are the president, treasurer and secretary?
3. **Rebels relationship.** Which model (1, 2 or 3 above) for a first test night — and who can agree it on Rebels' side?
4. **Gabriel's double role.** How do we handle decisions where Techonest and Rebels have different interests?
5. **The test night.** Rough date, venue type, size, ticket price, and the maximum we accept to lose.
6. **Money rules.** Who pays upfront costs, who signs, how any surplus is used.

The working plan lists the options, consequences and my recommendation for each.
