## A. Vision and boundaries

Phase 1 of Techonest is one thing only: co-producing a small number of culturally mixed nights in Paris that pay their artists and technicians. Everything else waits for evidence.

### Mission, vision, objectives

- **Mission (draft):** bring world cultures and art together on Paris stages — Arab music from the Middle East to Morocco, Afro, electronic and more — and give young, diverse technicians and artists paid, real work.
- **Long-term vision (draft):** a recognised community label that creates its own events and connects organisers with skilled event technicians.
- **Near-term objectives, to March 2027 (proposed):** (1) agree founders and structure; (2) run one test night; (3) learn real demand, costs and how working with Rebels feels.

### Problem, people served, value

| Who | Problem (hypothesis) | Proposed value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audiences in Paris | Few nights mix Arab, Afro and electronic scenes | A themed night with live music and DJs in one programme |
| Young technicians, DJs, musicians | Hard to get paid event work and references | Paid slots, credits, a portfolio and a network |
| Organisers and venues (later) | Finding reliable mixed crews at short notice | Coordinated technicians — only once legal set-up allows it |

### What Techonest offers first, and what it does not

| In phase 1 | Out of phase 1 |
| --- | --- |
| Co-producing 1 test night, possibly up to 6 events in the season | Three events a month |
| Programming live music next to DJs | Supplying technicians to other organisers for a margin |
| Coordinating the technical crew for our own nights | Buying equipment |
| Building a community list of artists and technicians | Taking investment or loans |

**Why supplying technicians is out of scope for now.** Lending workers to another business for profit is illegal in France unless you are a licensed temporary-work agency or another authorised case ([Code du travail L8241-1](https://code.travail.gouv.fr/code-du-travail/l8241-1)). Techonest could later introduce technicians who are hired or invoiced directly by the organiser, but that needs legal advice first.

### Techonest and Rebels

|  | Model 1 — Rebels produces | Model 2 — Techonest produces | Model 3 — Co-production |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Legal organiser | Rebels (needs a legal entity) | Techonest association | One named lead, by contract |
| Financial risk | Rebels | Techonest | Shared as agreed |
| Techonest gains | Experience, a fee or share, visibility, low risk | Control, learning, its own brand | A balance |
| Rebels gains | A live, cultural programme and a crew | A booking and audience growth | A partner sharing cost and work |
| Precondition | Rebels has a structure | Techonest association exists | Written agreement |

### Tensions to name

- **Commercial vs non-profit.** Ticketed nights are commercial activity; the association stays tax-exempt only while commercial receipts stay under 81 051 € a year and management stays disinterested ([BOFiP, 5 Aug 2026](https://bofip.impots.gouv.fr/node/381801)). Paying founders from it can endanger that status.
- **Gabriel on both sides.** A Techonest founder who is also Rebels can favour either side, without meaning to.
- **Mission vs individual income.** Oscar wants the project to help pay his studies; an association cannot share profits with its members.
- **Opportunity vs exploitation.** « Giving young technicians a chance » turns into unpaid labour quickly. The rule: paid nights pay everyone.

## C. Project model

In phase 1 the money comes from tickets, and possibly a bar share from the venue; the main costs are paying people and the venue. The technician-coordination service comes later and needs its own legal set-up.

### Services and who pays

| Service | Who pays | Phase | Legal note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Themed nights (tickets) | The audience | 1 | Organiser needs insurance, SACEM, GUSO payroll; a licence above 6 shows a year |
| Bar share or venue fee | The venue, by deal | 1 | Written venue contract |
| Technical crew for our own nights | Techonest pays them | 1 | Employees through GUSO, or genuine independents with their own business |
| Technical coordination for Rebels' nights | Rebels pays Techonest a fee | 1–2 | Techonest must be registered and invoice; crew still paid legally |
| Technician introductions for other organisers | The organiser | 2+ | No profit on lending workers ([L8241-1](https://code.travail.gouv.fr/code-du-travail/l8241-1)); legal advice first |
| Equipment rental | Organisers | 3+ | Needs owned gear, insurance; out of scope now |

### Partner journey, from first contact to follow-up

1. **Brief.** One page: concept, date range, size, budget, who does what.
2. **Rebels conversation.** Both Rebels founders; agree the model and the legal organiser.
3. **Venue.** Three meetings; written offer covering hire, bar share, capacity, security, SACEM and sound limits.
4. **Agreement.** A short co-production contract between the organiser and the partner.
5. **Line-up and crew.** Confirm artists and technicians; GUSO declarations before the night.
6. **Admin.** Insurance certificate, SACEM declaration, ticketing page.
7. **Promotion.** About four weeks of social media and community channels; track presales weekly.
8. **Go / stop check.** At day −10, compare presales with the threshold.
9. **The night.** Run sheet, safety briefing, one person in charge of security liaison.
10. **Settlement.** Within 7 days: receipts, costs, payments, SACEM, share between partners.
11. **Review.** Within 14 days: what worked, what to change, written decision on a second night.

### How the first night would work in practice (proposal)

- **Format:** one evening, live set (Ahmad's music and guests) followed by Rebels DJs, in a 150–250 capacity club or hall, between January and March 2027.
- **Organiser:** Techonest association, or Rebels if it is registered and prefers it.
- **Paid on the night (estimate):** 3–4 people through GUSO — artists and at least one technician. Other roles (door, welcome, promotion) may be volunteers only if the organiser is non-profit and they truly volunteer.
- **Ticket:** around 15 € (decision field).

### Viability and direction-change signals

**Signs it works (proposed thresholds):** presales reach the break-even line by day −10; the night ends at or above zero after everyone is paid; at least one venue wants a second date; the three founders still want to continue.

**Signs to change direction:** presales under about 60 % of break-even at day −10; costs above the cap before the night; Rebels cannot name a legal organiser; one of the founders cannot commit time. Then: switch to model 1 or 0, cut the line-up, or pause.

**Sensitivities.** If pay is 460 € per person instead of 300 €, the expected night falls to about −240 €. If the 5,5 % VAT rate applies (live paid artist, qualifying venue), the expected night rises to about +540 € and break-even falls to about 134 tickets. Your original target (3 nights a month × 300 × 15 €) was modelled earlier at between about −860 € and +3 600 € a month, and would exceed the association franchise and the six-show limit.

### Maximum at risk

Worst realistic case is about 1 200 € on the night plus up to 600 € of setup — close to 1 800 €, above the 1 000 € available. To cap the risk at 1 000 € (proposal):

- No venue hire; a bar-share deal with a cancellation clause.
- Presale check at day −10: if presales are under about 90 tickets (60 % of break-even), cancel or downsize within the venue's terms. Sunk cost at that point: about 300–500 € (marketing, insurance, any deposit).
- No equipment purchase; borrowed or rented gear only.
- Agree in writing who covers a loss: Techonest alone, or shared with Rebels.

| Someone injured | Safety | Low | High | Overcrowding, improvised rigging, no security plan | Venue contract, licensed security, insurance, trained crew on rigging |
| Gabriel's double role causes a dispute | Partnership | Medium | Medium | Disagreement over shares, credit or audience data | Written rule; he does not vote on Rebels deals; written co-production terms |
| Rebels declines or changes terms late | Partnership | Medium | Medium | Slow replies; Play House not involved | Talk to both founders; keep model 2 ready |
| A founder cannot give the time | Staffing | Medium | Medium | Missed weekly calls, tasks slipping | Agree hours now; scope down |
| Costs exceed the cap | Financial | Medium | High | Quotes above estimates | Cut line-up, no hire fee, or postpone |
| School withdraws equipment or students | Operational | Medium | Medium | No written answer from EICAR | Get it in writing; have a rental quote as backup |
| Wrong VAT rate or unpaid SACEM / CNM | Legal, financial | Medium | Medium | No written confirmation before the night | Ask the venue, SACEM and an accountant beforehand |
| A bad first night hurts the name | Reputational | Low–Medium | Medium | Rushed promotion, weak line-up | Launch only after Gate C; small room that fills |

### Weakest assumptions

1. That demand exists at 15 € for this mix — untested.
2. That Rebels is, or can quickly become, a legal organiser — unknown.
3. That a venue will waive hire — untested.
4. That everyone can be paid legally and the night still breaks even — only true in the expected case with low pay estimates.

### The strongest arguments against proceeding now

- On the estimates, even a good test night earns a few hundred euros; it will not pay Oscar's fees this year.
- Oscar's titre limits what he can legally run and earn, which shifts real control and risk onto others.
- Rebels' public activity appears thin after October 2025 on the sources checked; its current capacity is unknown.

| Technical coordination for one Rebels night, paid as a service | Low | Whether Techonest's crew role has value to organisers |
