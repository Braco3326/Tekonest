## Summary

The lowest-risk way to start in 2026–27 is a **Techonest association loi 1901** that co-organises a small number of events with Rebels, with every paid artist and technician on a proper payslip. But five parts of the current plan would put you, your titre de séjour or the project at real risk, and they need fixing before any structure is chosen.

**The five red flags**

1. **Your student titre de séjour blocks the micro-entreprise.** Service-Public states that a student in France on a student titre de séjour cannot combine student and micro-entrepreneur status ([source](https://www.justice.fr/fiche/cumuler-statuts-etudiant-micro-entrepreneur)). Running a company (SAS, SARL) as its legal head also normally needs a titre that allows commercial activity — this needs a lawyer's confirmation for your exact titre.
2. **« Bénévolat after 964 h » is the riskiest idea in the plan.** Volunteers are only allowed in non-profit structures; no volunteer can work in a commercial structure ([DREETS](https://corse.dreets.gouv.fr/sites/corse.dreets.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/benevolat.pdf)). Paying volunteers a percentage is pay, not reimbursement, and courts requalify that as an employment contract and travail dissimulé ([Associathèque](https://www.associatheque.fr/fr/actualite-attention-au-pseudo-benevolat.html)). For you personally, regular unpaid work in your own event project beyond your 964 h could be read as hidden work at your titre renewal — my judgment, to confirm with an immigration lawyer.
3. **Three events a month makes you a habitual show organiser.** Above six performances a year, the licence (déclaration d'entrepreneur de spectacles) is mandatory ([Artcena](https://www.artcena.fr/guide/droit-et-administration/creer-et-gerer-sa-compagnie/declaration-dactivite-dentrepreneur-de-spectacles-vivants)), the simple GUSO payroll for occasional organisers is no longer open to you ([Associathèque](https://www.associatheque.fr/fr/fichiers/focus/Focus-Le-recours-aux-intermittents.pdf)), and paid performers (DJs, singers, musicians) are presumed employees ([Unédic](https://www.unedic.org/ged/documents/circulars/pdf/PRE-CIRC-1483982112090.pdf)).
4. **The revenue target breaks every light regime.** 3 × 300 × 15 € = 13 500 € a month, about 162 000 € a year. That is twice the 2026 tax franchise for associations (81 051 €, [BOFiP](https://bofip.impots.gouv.fr/node/381801)) and twice the micro-entreprise ceiling (83 600 €, [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/entreprises/micro-entreprise-auto-entreprise-charges-sociales)). And after VAT, rights, venue and legal pay, a 300-ticket night at 15 € can end near zero (see Numbers check).
5. **Gabriel (GrooveBox) is a co-founder of Rebels.** If he also co-founds Techonest, every deal between the two is with himself. That works only with a written agreement on who decides, who pays and who keeps what.

**Suggested starting setup (my judgment, details below):** an association with you as unpaid president and Ahmad on the board; a first season of at most six events, co-produced with Rebels; artists and technicians paid through GUSO; no volunteers on paid jobs; revisit a company (SAS) in 2027 when your titre de séjour changes.

## Legal structures in France, explained

France has three families of structure that matter here: an **association** (non-profit), the **entreprise individuelle** (you alone, no separate company) and **companies** (SAS, SARL and their one-person versions). Several names you hear are tax regimes, not separate structures.

**Names that describe a regime, not a structure**

- **Micro-entreprise = auto-entreprise.** Same thing: an entreprise individuelle taxed on turnover with flat social contributions. « EI au réel » is the same legal entity under normal accounting.
- **EURL** is a one-person SARL; **SASU** is a one-person SAS. Same rules, one partner.
- **CAE** (coopérative d'activité et d'emploi) is not something you create: you join an existing cooperative that invoices for you and pays you as a salaried « entrepreneur-salarié ».

### Thresholds that change the picture

- **Association tax franchise: 81 051 € of commercial receipts in 2026.** Under it, a disinterested, mainly non-profit association pays no corporate tax, CFE or VAT; above it, the commercial part becomes taxable ([BOFiP, 5 Aug 2026](https://bofip.impots.gouv.fr/node/381801)). One site still quotes 72 431 €; the official figure is 81 051 €.
- **Micro-entreprise ceiling: 83 600 €** of services a year for 2026–28 ([economie.gouv.fr, Mar 2026](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/entreprises/micro-entreprise-auto-entreprise-charges-sociales)).
- **VAT franchise for services: 37 500 €** (41 250 € in the year it is passed); the 2026 finance law left it unchanged ([CCI Lyon](https://www.lyon-metropole.cci.fr/actualite/micro-entreprises-le-projet-de-loi-de-finances-2026-relance-la-reforme-du-seuil-de)).

### Strengths and traps for an events business

- **Association.** Best fit for a cultural, social mission, volunteers and school partners; can sell tickets and hire artists. Traps: if paid events become the main activity, it loses its non-profit tax status; volunteers cannot do jobs others are paid for; you do not own it, so you cannot sell it later.
- **SAS.** Best for a real business with partners, investors and a technician agency; easy to add shareholders. Traps: no volunteers at all; accounting from day one; likely closed to you until your titre changes.
- **SARL.** Similar to SAS but more rigid; little reason to prefer it here.
- **CAE.** Lets you test billing for tech and DJ services without your own company. Traps: it is for selling your own services, not for running ticketed events; check that a cooperative accepts a student titre.

## Recommendation

Start Techonest as an association loi 1901 for 2026–27, run a test season of at most six co-produced events with Rebels, and decide on a company in 2027 once your titre de séjour and the real numbers are known.

**Legal facts it rests on**

- A student titre excludes micro-entreprise; salaried work up to 964 h is allowed.
- Volunteers are legal only in a non-profit, and only with expenses refunded.
- At or under six performances a year, no licence is needed and GUSO handles payroll.
- Under 81 051 € of commercial receipts, a disinterested association pays no commercial taxes.

**How it would work (my judgment)**

1. **Techonest association**: you as unpaid president, Ahmad as treasurer or secretary. Gabriel joins only with a written note on his double role, or stays on the Rebels side.
2. **Each event has one named organiser.** Either Rebels (if it has a structure and licence) produces and Techonest provides the cultural programme and technical coordination; or Techonest organises and Rebels is a paid DJ partner. A one-page co-production agreement says who sells tickets, pays SACEM, holds the insurance and keeps what share.
3. **Everyone performing or working on a paid night is paid through GUSO.** That includes you, inside your 964 h, which also builds your 2027 titre file.
4. **Volunteers only for genuinely voluntary roles** (welcome desk, community, promotion), never for paid technical or artistic jobs, and never on a percentage.
5. **School gear only with written permission** and insurance cover.

**The strongest argument against it.** An association is not yours: you cannot own it, sell it or take its profits, and a board can outvote you. If the events succeed at three a month, it becomes a taxable, licensed business anyway, and you will restructure within a year.

**What would make another option better**

- **SAS** if your titre allows running a company (EU nationality, an APS or a talent card), or if investors or a real technician-agency business appear.
- **CAE** if most of your income turns out to be your own DJ and tech services rather than ticketed events.
- **Organising nothing yourself** — working as a paid DJ or technician for Rebels' events — if the numbers check stays negative after real quotes. That is the zero-risk way to test demand.

**What is still missing before you decide:** your nationality and exact titre mention, Rebels' legal status, real quotes for one venue, and EICAR's answer on equipment.
